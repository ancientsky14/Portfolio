## What it is

One system, three front doors. The public gets a portal for ordinances, council
sessions and transparency records. The staff who publish get an admin CMS with
role-based access. Everyone inside the office gets an intranet on its own hostname.
Same data, three deployments, three very different definitions of "usable."

## Constraints

- **Three audiences with nothing in common.** A resident on a phone with one bar and a
  clerk on a decade-old desktop cannot be served by the same interface. Trying to do it
  in one app is how these projects turn into something nobody uses.
- **Connectivity.** Santol is a fourth-class inland municipality in La Union. Designing
  for Makati bandwidth would have produced a system that fails exactly when the office
  is busiest.
- **Municipal budget.** Hosting cost is a recurring line item that has to survive an
  annual budget review, which makes predictable near-zero cost worth more than raw speed.
- **Staff turnover.** Same constraint as every LGU engagement: the system has to be
  learnable by the next person without me.

## What I built

Three separately deployed Next.js applications over one shared Supabase project, with
Postgres row-level security as the boundary between them — the public surface physically
cannot read what only the admin surface should see, because the database refuses, not
because the UI hides a button.

Splitting the deployments rather than gating routes inside one app means the public
surface stays small and fast, and a change to the admin tooling cannot take the
resident-facing site down. It also means each surface can be scaled, cached, and
rolled back on its own.
